Six senses. One home.

House of Six takes its name from an old idea: a house built around beauty and daily ritual. We believe a home works the same way, through sight, scent, taste, touch, sound, and the sixth sense that ties them all together.

The House

Where the name comes from.

In astrology, the Sixth House sits under Venus's rule, the house of daily life, beauty, and the small rituals that shape how we live. Less grand gesture, more everyday: the scent that lingers in a doorway, the weight of a good ceramic cup, the light through a window at 6pm. That's the house we build for.

A home isn't furnished in one sense. It's felt in six. Sight, in the objects you choose. Scent, in the air you keep. Taste, in what your kitchen holds. Touch, in the textures underfoot. Sound, in the quiet a room allows. And the sixth: the sense that tells you a room is right before you can say why.

The Founder

Fourth generation. First homecoming.

I left home at eighteen, moving from Delhi to Pittsburgh to study business and mathematics at Carnegie Mellon University. That was only the beginning. Five years at Microsoft took me to Seattle and then Vancouver, and an MBA at MIT Sloan brought me to Boston after that. Five cities, two continents, and never one address I called home for long.

Every one of those cities was rented. None of them were meant to be forever. So I stopped buying furniture and started buying the things that fit in a suitcase: cups, throws, small vases, plants I could carry to the next place. All in the shades that made a room feel like mine, wherever I was staying.

That instinct, the one that could turn a rented studio into a home in an afternoon, is the actual design brief behind House of Six. Every piece is made to be classic, not seasonal: silver, gold, rose gold, antique, so a lease, a trend, or a price tag is never the reason a home stays half-finished.

It's also four generations in the making. My family spent three generations taking Indian craftsmanship out into the world: pieces made by hand, sent everywhere but home. I spent mine moving through five cities on two continents, learning the hard way what makes a rented room feel like yours. House of Six is where both stories land: my family's hands, and my own restlessness, finally building for the same house.

"AI can plan a room. It can't tell you it feels right. That's still ours."

It was at Sloan, immersed in AI, that the gap became obvious to me: intelligence can plan a room, lay out a floor plan, optimize a budget. But it can't smell a candle before you light it, or know a room is right before you can say why. That gap is the six senses. It's also the closest thing I've found to instinct itself: the same instinct that filled a dozen rented rooms, one accessory at a time.
